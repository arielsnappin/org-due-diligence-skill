## Bottom line

Company A is the only option on your list that puts you in the top product seat within two years. The offer is Director of Product, with an exec-team seat from day one, reporting to the founder-CEO [Verified · offer letter]. The catch is that you'd get the job before the title. "Grows into Head of Product" is the recruiter's phrase, not the offer letter's [Reported · recruiter], and no Head of Product band is posted.

The bigger question isn't the title but whether the founder will actually hand over product. The last Head of Product lasted 11 months, and in 2025 the founder said they still read every spec. That's the risk to price, and you can find out before Thursday.

## Who runs product, and who you'd work for

The founder-CEO ran product at a large analytics company before starting Company A and still runs the weekly product reviews [Reported · podcast, 2025]. You'd be the first product leader between them and the PMs.

Compare that with Company B, where you'd report to a VP of Product with three years in seat and a record of promoting PMs. At Company A there's no product leader above you to learn from: your manager is the founder, and the founder is the product's original author.

## What stands out

**The seat has turned over once, and the exit was sideways.** The previous Head of Product left after 11 months for a Director role at a larger company [Reported · LinkedIn]. That's a lateral move, not a poach to a bigger title [Inferred · dates and titles]. A lateral exit after a short tenure usually means the job wasn't what was promised. Ask what happened, and ask someone other than the founder.

**The founder still reads every spec.** They said so publicly in 2025 [Reported · podcast]. Plenty of founders say this and then let go; some don't. Together with the 11-month exit, it's the single most important thing to test.

**The engineering bench is the founder's old team.** Three of the four engineering leads came from the founder's previous company [Reported · LinkedIn]. That usually means fast alignment and high trust. It also means a harder road for an outsider walking into a tight group.

## Org shape and where the seats are

The product and design team grew from 6 to 11 in a year [Reported · LinkedIn, function-filtered; base 6]: 4 PMs, 5 designers, and 2 researchers. You'd lead the 4 PMs [Verified · offer letter]. Whether design reports to you isn't stated, which makes it either scope you'd gain or a turf question you'd inherit. The only layer above you is the founder, so "the next seat" is Head of Product itself, and it exists only if the founder creates it.

The recruiter described one of the four PMs, a Senior PM with three years at the company, as "acting product lead since last fall" [Reported · recruiter]. They are very likely an internal candidate for your role [Inferred]. That isn't a reason to say no. It's a relationship to plan for from day one.

## Fit for you

Your analytics product line is the closest match to Company A's core product on any board you're looking at, so you'd be credible with their customers on day one. On scope it's a sideways step (4 PMs instead of your 3). On altitude it's a real step up: an exec-team seat, direct access to the CEO, and ownership of the whole roadmap rather than one line of it.

**The strategic ask:** get the path to Head of Product in writing before you sign, meaning the criteria and a review date. It's a normal ask now. After you've started, it becomes a negotiation.

## Risks

**The founder doesn't let go.** If the founder keeps running reviews, you'd have the title and the exec seat without the authority, which is the most likely reason the last person left. Ask the founder which decisions they'll hand over (see the Call Sheet), and ask the previous Head of Product.

**The internal candidate.** A passed-over Senior PM who's close to the founder can make your first six months hard. Ask the founder whether they've already talked with that PM about this hire.

**The office policy is unknown, and it's a must-have for you.** Postings say "NYC-based" without a policy. Ask the recruiter this week.

**Runway.** The Series B ($38M) closed 14 months ago [Verified · press release]. Ask about runway and timing for the next raise before treating the equity as meaningful.
