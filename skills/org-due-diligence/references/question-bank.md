# Question bank

Pick 3–5 questions per conversation, starting with the ones that could change the decision. Tailor each one with what you know. Every question has a **listen-for** (what a real answer sounds like versus a non-answer), the **dimension** it informs, and **when** it's best asked.

## Contents
- Recruiter
- Hiring manager
- Skip-level / functional leader
- Founder / CEO
- Peer
- Prospective report
- Backchannel (current or former employee)
- At the offer: get it in writing

---

## Recruiter
Gatekeeper and logistics. Low stakes, so ask the "boring" questions here.

**"Is this a new role or a backfill? If it's a backfill, what happened to the person before?"**
- Real answer: a clear, unembarrassed story (they were promoted or moved teams).
- Non-answer: "They moved on." Follow up with the hiring manager or a backchannel.
- Dimension: Scope & team · When: before or during the loop

**"What level is this role calibrated at, and could I be considered at [next level]?"**
- Real answer: they explain how leveling is decided and who decides it.
- Non-answer: "The level is fixed," with no explanation of the rubric.
- Dimension: Path to next level · When: **before the loop.** It's free now and expensive after the offer.

**"What's the band for this level, and where do offers usually land within it?"**
- Real answer: a number and a range.
- Non-answer: "We're competitive."
- Dimension: Economics · When: before the loop or at the offer

**"Who's on the loop, and who makes the final call?"**
- Real answer: names and roles, and whether it's one decider or a committee.
- Dimension: Leadership & exec access · When: before the loop

## Hiring manager
The decider and the future boss. Most of the ★ answers live here.

**"What does success look like at 6 and 18 months, and how will we both know?"**
- Real answer: named outcomes or metrics.
- Non-answer: "Make an impact," "own the roadmap."
- Dimension: Scope & team

**"What's the biggest thing that isn't working today that you'd want me to take on?"**
- Real answer: a candid diagnosis.
- Non-answer: "Honestly, things are going great."
- Dimension: Scope & team, Org health

**"What would your current reports say is the best part and the hardest part of working for you?"**
- Real answer: a self-aware answer that includes a real weakness.
- Non-answer: no weakness, or a humble-brag.
- Dimension: Manager

**"Tell me about someone you've promoted. What did it take?"**
- Real answer: a named example with specific criteria.
- Non-answer: no example, or "I'm new here" with nothing from previous roles.
- Dimension: Manager, Path to next level

**"What would the path from [level] to [next level] look like here, and on what timeline?"**
- Real answer: names a cycle, specific gaps, and a rough date.
- Non-answer: "You'd be doing great, we'd revisit," with enthusiasm but no mechanism.
- Dimension: Path to next level · When: during the loop or at the offer

**"Who owns the decisions in this area today, and what changes when I join?"**
- Real answer: clear decision rights, and who gives something up.
- Non-answer: "We're very collaborative."
- Dimension: Scope & team, Leadership

**"Was anyone on the team considered for this role? How are you thinking about that?"** *(people-manager roles)*
- Real answer: acknowledges it and has a plan.
- Non-answer: dismisses it, or looks surprised by the question.
- Dimension: Scope & team

**"How is your own role likely to change over the next year?"**
- Real answer: stable, or a change that's known and planned.
- Non-answer: vagueness, which can be a sign of a reorg or a departure.
- Dimension: Manager

## Skip-level / functional leader

**"How is product represented at the exec table, and where does this role fit?"**
- Real answer: a clear line to the exec team, and a forum this role sits in.
- Dimension: Leadership & exec access

**"What are the one or two company bets this team's work ladders up to?"**
- Real answer: crisp and consistent with what the hiring manager said.
- Non-answer: a mismatch with the hiring manager. That's worth noting.
- Dimension: Scope & team, Leadership

**"What's changed in the product org over the last year, and what do you expect to change next?"**
- Real answer: candid about reorgs and exits.
- Non-answer: "Not much," when you know there were changes.
- Dimension: Org health

**"What distinguishes the people who've grown fastest here?"**
- Real answer: specific behaviors or examples.
- Dimension: Path to next level

## Founder / CEO

**"Where do you stay close to product decisions, and where do you want to let go?"**
- Real answer: specific areas they'll delegate.
- Non-answer: "I trust the team," while evidence suggests they override it.
- Dimension: Leadership & exec access

**"What's the one thing that could stop this company in the next two years?"**
- Real answer: clear-eyed about a real risk.
- Non-answer: pure cheerleading.
- Dimension: Org health

**"How are you thinking about runway and the next milestone, whether a funding round or profitability?"**
- Dimension: Org health, Economics · When: at the offer

**"A few leaders in [area] changed over the last year. What did you learn from that?"** *(only if the changes are public)*
- Real answer: owns the lessons.
- Non-answer: blames the people who left.
- Dimension: Org health, Leadership

## Peer

- "What's it like to work with [hiring manager]? Who thrives with them?" *(Manager)*
- "How do decisions get made between your team and this role?" *(Scope & team)*
- "Who's been promoted in the product org recently, and how?" *(Path to next level)*
- "What would you tell a friend to look into before joining?" *(any)*

## Prospective report
Be thoughtful here: one of them may have wanted the role.

- "What do you need from your next manager that you haven't had?" *(Scope & team)*
- "What's the best part of the team right now, and the hardest?" *(Scope & team, Org health)*

## Backchannel (current or former employee)
Respect their time and confidentiality. Say "share only what you're comfortable with."

- "What's [manager] like to work for? Who does well with them, and who doesn't?" *(Manager)*
- "Why did you leave?" or "Why do people tend to leave?" *(Org health)*
- "How does promotion really work there?" *(Path to next level)*
- "If a friend were considering this role, what would you tell them to ask about?" *(any)*

## At the offer: get it in writing
Clarify these, or ask for them in the offer letter:
- title and level;
- reporting line;
- scope, team, and headcount;
- location policy;
- start date;
- the details of any promise made about promotion timing or future headcount. Promises said out loud stay "Reported" until they're in writing.
