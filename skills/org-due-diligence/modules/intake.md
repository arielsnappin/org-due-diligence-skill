# Intake

**Goal:** learn just enough to be useful, in one message that takes about three minutes and where every question is skippable.

Intake exists because an early version of this skill only discovered the user's real question ("what's my path to director?") halfway through, so everything before that point was framed wrong. But an interrogation is worse than a late question, so keep intake light.

## Before you ask

Read what the user has already told you, plus any `runs/*/profile.md` from an earlier session. Ask only for what's missing. If you already have enough to start (for example a company, a role, and what they care about), skip to confirming priorities and showing the menu.

## The message

Send one message. You can adapt the wording, but keep the substance, and keep the promise that everything is skippable:

> Happy to help you look at the org behind the offer. A few quick questions first. This takes about 3 minutes, and **everything is skippable**: answer what you can, say "skip", or just give me a company name and I'll start.
>
> 1. **What are you weighing?** Company, role, and level for each option. Include staying where you are if that's on the table.
> 2. **Where are you now?** Your title, scope, and team, in a sentence.
> 3. **What do you want this move to do for you?** For example a title, more scope, AI experience, money, or stability. What does success look like in 2–5 years?
> 4. **Any deal-breakers?** For example location, reporting to a founder, or no path to management.
> 5. **What's your timeline?** Your offer deadline and your next scheduled conversation.
> 6. **Anything I should read?** Paste the JD, the offer, or your notes from recruiter calls.
>
> [LinkedIn note, see below]

Drop any question the user has already answered. Don't add new ones. Anything else you need, you'll learn along the way.

## The LinkedIn note

Most of the best signal (who they'd report to, who has left, who they know there) comes from LinkedIn. Many users won't know what a browser extension is, so keep this plain.

**If you don't have a browser tool**, include:

> **One setup step for the LinkedIn part:** to look at LinkedIn with you, I need the **Claude in Chrome** extension (on paid Claude plans).
> 1. Install "Claude" from the Chrome Web Store and sign in.
> 2. In the Claude desktop app, open **Settings → Connectors**, turn on **Claude in Chrome**, then switch it on in this chat's connectors menu. (In Claude Code, start with `claude --chrome` or run `/chrome`.)
> 3. Make sure you're logged in to LinkedIn in Chrome.
>
> I'll only look. I'll never message or connect with anyone. If you'd rather skip this, I'll use public sources and tell you the two or three things worth checking yourself.

**If you have a browser tool**, open linkedin.com once to confirm the user is logged in. If a login page appears, ask them to log in themselves. Never type credentials.

**Profile-view heads-up.** Before any LinkedIn research, tell the user once:

> "LinkedIn may show people that you viewed their profile. If you'd rather stay anonymous, switch to private mode first, under Settings → Visibility → Profile viewing options."

This is their setting to change. Don't change it for them.

## Infer priorities (no forms, no weights)

Map what the user said they want onto the seven dimensions in `references/dimensions.md`, which includes a goal-to-dimension table. Pick the top 2–3, mark them ★, and confirm in one line:

> "I'm reading your priorities as ★ path to the next level and ★ your manager. Right?"

If they correct you, update. If they don't answer, go with your read and say that you did.

**Deal-breakers are gates, not priorities.** Keep them as a separate list. Each one gets checked as pass, fail, or unknown before any rating.

## When answers are skipped

Proceed on sensible assumptions and name them in one line ("I'll assume staying isn't an option. Tell me if it is."). Never block on intake.

## Save and move on

If you can write files, save `profile.md` in the run folder with: the options (including stay), current role, goals, ★ priorities, deal-breakers, timeline and next conversation, and a list of materials received. It's private and stays local.

Then show the menu (see `SKILL.md`) with your suggested starting point.
