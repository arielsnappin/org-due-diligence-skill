# Rating dimensions

Seven dimensions, each rated **High / Medium / Low / ?** *relative to the user's goals*. A Medium can be fine for a dimension the user doesn't care about. A Low on a ★ dimension is a big deal.

- **High:** a real strength for this person's goals.
- **Medium:** okay, or mixed.
- **Low:** a real weakness or risk.
- **?:** we don't know yet. Worth finding out if the dimension is ★.

## 1. Manager & reporting line
*Will this person grow me and advocate for me, and will they still be here in a year?*

**Look at:**
- how long they've been in seat and at the company;
- whether they've operated at the level above the user;
- whether their past reports got promoted;
- how stable their own reporting line is (a new boss above them? a recent reorg?);
- how clearly they describe the role;
- what backchannel contacts say.

| Rating | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **High** | Has done the job above the user's. In seat long enough to be established (roughly 1–4 years). Visible track record of growing and promoting people. Stable line above them. |
| **Medium** | Capable, but new to the level or the company (less than ~6 months), or there's little evidence either way about how they develop people. |
| **Low** | The seat keeps turning over (theirs or the user's), they're very new *and* have a new boss, their reports keep leaving, or the user would report to someone with less scope than they have today. |

**Red flags:** the reporting line changed during the process; "dotted-line" ambiguity; nobody in the loop can say who the user would really report to.

## 2. Leadership & exec access
*How strong is the bench above me, how much does product matter here, and do I get a seat at the table?*

**Look at:**
- backgrounds and tenure of the functional leader and the exec team;
- whether product reports to the CEO or through a CTO or COO;
- whether this role sits in a leadership group or staff meeting;
- how involved the founders are in product decisions;
- how stable the exec team has been.

| Rating | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **High** | Product is represented at the exec table, the leadership bench is strong and stable, and this role is part of the leadership forum. |
| **Medium** | Product reports through another function, or the bench is strong but the role sits two or more layers away from it. |
| **Low** | Exec churn, a founder who overrides product decisions, or product treated as a delivery function. |

**Red flags:**
- several C-level departures in 12 months;
- a CPO or VP Product seat left vacant for a long time;
- for a Head of Product role, a founder who is still the de facto CPO.

## 3. Path to next level
*Where does my next title or scope come from, and does that seat exist?*

**Look at:**
- level definitions and the posted bands (do IC bands reach the manager bands?);
- how many next-level seats exist, and who holds them;
- whether those seats get filled internally or from outside;
- how fast the function is growing, since new seats appear when orgs grow;
- the hiring manager's answer to "what would it take?"

| Rating | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **High** | A named next-level seat exists or is budgeted, internal promotions are visible, and the criteria are clear. |
| **Medium** | The path exists in principle but depends on growth or on someone leaving, and the timeline is vague. |
| **Low** | Next-level seats are filled from outside, the org is flat or shrinking, or there's no ladder above the user's level. |

## 4. Scope & team
*What will I actually own, and whom will I lead or work alongside?*

**Look at:**
- the surface, users, or P&L the role owns;
- decision rights;
- team size, seniority, and tenure;
- whether there's an internal candidate on the team, and the predecessor's story;
- resourcing (engineering and design support);
- peers whose scope overlaps.

| Rating | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **High** | Clear, meaningful scope with resourcing, a healthy team, and peers whose work complements the role. |
| **Medium** | The scope is real but fuzzy at the edges or under-resourced. |
| **Low** | Scope overlaps with a peer or a founder, a passed-over internal candidate is on the team with no plan for it, or the predecessor left quickly. |

## 5. Org health & momentum
*Is the org growing and keeping its people?*

**Look at:**
- function-level hiring trend (always with the base count);
- funding recency and runway;
- revenue signals;
- layoffs or reorgs;
- clusters of exits;
- leadership tenure.

| Rating | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **High** | The function is growing, funding is recent or the company is profitable, and leadership churn is low. |
| **Medium** | Flat but stable, or growing on funding that's getting stale. |
| **Low** | The function is shrinking, or there have been layoffs, a cluster of exits, or stale funding alongside visible burn. |

## 6. Economics
*How does the package compare, and how real is the equity?*

**Look at:**
- the offer against the posted bands for the level and location;
- equity type and company stage;
- the last round and its date;
- tender or liquidity history;
- refresh policy.

Surface the questions to ask. **Never value equity or give financial advice.**

| Rating | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **High** | At or above the band for the level, with a recent round or a liquidity history. |
| **Medium** | Within the band, but the equity is hard to read. |
| **Low** | Below the band, a stale valuation, or no liquidity path in sight. |

## 7. Ways of working
*Will I do my best work here day to day?*

**Look at:**
- location policy;
- pace and intensity (executives' public posts, stated values);
- decision-making style;
- review-site themes, at low confidence only.

| Rating | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **High** | Matches what the user said they want. |
| **Medium** | Some friction, but it could be managed. |
| **Low** | Conflicts with something they said matters, e.g., five days on-site when they want hybrid. If it's a deal-breaker, it's a gate instead. |

## Inferring ★ priorities from goals

Mark the top 2–3 dimensions as ★ and confirm them in one line. Never ask the user for weights.

| If they say something like… | ★ this dimension |
|---|---|
| "title", "promotion", "next level", "director", "VP" | Path to next level |
| "learn", "mentor", "grow", "a great boss" | Manager & reporting line |
| "influence", "strategy", "seat at the table", "exec path" | Leadership & exec access |
| "build a team", "own something bigger", "impact", "AI work", "new domain" | Scope & team |
| "stability", "runway", "a winner", "IPO" | Org health & momentum |
| "money", "equity", "comp" | Economics |
| "flexibility", "remote", "pace", "balance", "culture" | Ways of working |

If their goals are vague, lean toward Manager and Path to next level. For senior people, those two usually decide the next five years.

## Deal-breakers (gates)

A deal-breaker is pass/fail, not a weight, so it can't be averaged away. Common ones:
- location or on-site requirements;
- reporting to a founder, or not reporting to one;
- a minimum level or title;
- a people-management requirement (must manage, or must not);
- base pay below a floor;
- industry or mission exclusions.

Check each one as ✓, ✗, or ? for every option, before any rating.
